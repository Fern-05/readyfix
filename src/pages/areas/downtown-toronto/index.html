Condos, lofts and century homes, often on the same street

Downtown work changes from one address to the next. A CityPlace or Fort York condo usually means concrete ceilings, steel-stud partition walls and a management office with rules about elevators and working hours. A King West or Liberty Village loft might mean exposed brick and old timber. A few blocks away, a Cabbagetown or Annex house has plaster-and-lath walls, original trim and doors that have shifted over a hundred years.

We plan for each of those before we arrive. For condos, we confirm the elevator booking with management, protect the hallway and floors, and control dust with HEPA extraction at the source. For older houses, we bring anchors that suit plaster and brick, and we take our time around original woodwork.

What that means on the day

  • Mounting into concrete takes a hammer drill and concrete anchors, so we bring both and keep the noisy part inside building hours.
  • Steel studs don't hold screws the way wood does. TVs and shelves go up on anchors rated for the wall, not whatever came in the box.
  • Plaster cracks around careless drilling. We pre-drill, look for solid backing and fill old anchor holes as we go.
  • Parking and loading are tight downtown. Let us know about visitor parking or loading dock rules when you book.

Most requested downtown

The repairs downtown homes need most

  • TV & artwork mounting

    Condo walls are concrete or steel stud, and loft walls are often brick. Each needs different hardware, so we check the wall first and mount with anchors rated for the load.

  • Curtain rod installation

    Floor-to-ceiling condo windows often mean drilling into a concrete ceiling or bulkhead. We anchor rods and tracks properly so they stay level and don't pull out.

  • Drywall repair

    Plumbing access holes, bulkhead dents and leak damage from the unit above are common in towers. We patch, texture-match and prime with dust kept inside your unit.

  • Punch lists

    Downtown condos sell on their photos. We clear pre-listing touch-ups and move-out repairs in one visit so the listing and the closing stay on schedule.

  • Cabinet repair

    Compact condo kitchens get heavy use. Sagging doors, loose hinges and worn drawer slides are quick fixes, and soft-close hinges are an easy upgrade.

  • Doors & trim

    In Victorian and Edwardian houses, doors bind as frames settle and trim opens up at the joints. We adjust, plane, re-secure and caulk without losing the original character.

FAQ

Downtown Toronto handyman questions

Do you handle condo elevator bookings?

Yes. Some buildings let us book directly with management or concierge, and others need the resident to book through the building's system. Either way, we give you a firm arrival window to book against and confirm it before the visit.

Can you mount a TV on a condo wall?

Usually, yes. Concrete walls get concrete anchors, and steel-stud drywall walls get heavy-duty anchors rated for the TV and mount. We check the wall before drilling, and if a spot won't hold safely, we'll tell you and suggest another. It's also worth checking whether your building has rules about drilling into concrete or shared walls.

Do you work on older downtown houses with plaster walls?

Yes. We drill plaster carefully to avoid cracking, find solid backing for anything heavy, and patch cracks and old holes so they're paint-ready. If a large area of plaster has come loose from the lath, we'll explain the options honestly rather than patch over a problem.

Can you do the work while I'm at the office?

Yes. Many downtown clients leave a key with concierge or use a lockbox. We send before and after photos when the work is done, and invoice online.
