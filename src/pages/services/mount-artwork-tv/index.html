Common challenges

Tricky walls and mounts, and how we handle them

  • Concrete condo walls

    Many condo walls are poured concrete or block. We drill with masonry bits and use concrete screws or sleeve anchors rated for the load, vacuuming at the drill so dust doesn't spread.

  • Steel studs

    Steel studs don't hold wood screws. We locate them and use anchors made for metal studs or hollow walls, sized for the weight and for the extra leverage a full-motion arm puts on the wall.

  • Plaster and lath

    Older plaster cracks if it's drilled carelessly. We find the studs behind it, pre-drill, and use fasteners long enough to reach solid framing rather than relying on the plaster.

  • Gallery walls

    We plan the arrangement first, on the floor or with paper templates, set a consistent centre height and spacing, then hang each piece with hardware suited to its weight.

  • Heavy mirrors

    Large mirrors go onto studs or masonry with a French cleat, D-rings or mirror clips rated for the weight, never a single nail or picture hook.

  • Messy cables

    Cables run through a paintable cord cover or, where the wall allows, through the cavity behind recessed low-voltage plates. Power cords stay on the existing outlet.

Our process

How we mount your TV or artwork

  1. Plan placement

    We confirm height, centring and clearances with you, and check the bracket or hanging hardware against the weight.

  2. Find the structure

    Studs, steel framing or concrete are located and marked, and we scan for obstructions before drilling.

  3. Mount & level

    Brackets go up with anchors rated for the load, then the TV or artwork is hung, levelled and secured.

  4. Tidy & test

    Cables are dressed, test holes patched and dust vacuumed, and we test tilt or swivel with you before we leave.

FAQ

TV and artwork mounting questions

Do you provide the TV bracket?

We can supply a common TV bracket or install the one you provide. Either way, we check that it fits your TV's mounting pattern and that its weight rating suits the screen.

Can you hide the wires?

We offer paintable cord covers or, where feasible, in-wall routing for low-voltage cables like HDMI and speaker wire using code-compliant methods. We'll confirm options on site. If you want a new outlet behind the TV, that's a job for a licensed electrician.

Can you mount a TV on a concrete condo wall?

Yes. We use masonry bits and anchors rated for concrete, protect floors, mind building noise rules, and vacuum thoroughly after drilling. We confirm elevator bookings with management when needed.

How high should my TV be mounted?

A common guideline is to put the centre of the screen near seated eye level, often around 100 to 110 cm (40 to 43 inches) from the floor in a living room. Above a fireplace or in a bedroom it usually goes higher, with a tilting bracket to angle the screen down. We'll mark the spot with you before drilling.

How heavy a mirror or piece of art can you hang?

It depends on the wall more than the piece. Fastened into studs or concrete with the right hardware, large and heavy mirrors are routine. On hollow drywall we choose anchors rated well above the weight. Let us know the size and rough weight when you ask for a quote.

What should I have ready before you arrive?

Have the bracket and its hardware on hand if you bought one, decide roughly where the TV and any cable box or soundbar will sit, and clear furniture from the wall. For gallery walls, have all the pieces there so we can plan the layout together.
