Straight rods that stay put

A curtain rod carries more load than it looks: the weight of the panels plus a tug every time they're opened and closed. Brackets screwed into bare drywall with the plastic plugs from the box tend to loosen and pull out, especially under blackout or lined curtains. The fix is putting brackets where there's solid material, or using anchors rated for the wall.

ReadyFix measures twice, lays out brackets so the rod is centred and level, and hits studs or uses the right anchors for drywall, concrete or masonry. Many Toronto condo windows have concrete or a steel lintel right where the brackets go; we drill those cleanly and vacuum the dust. If you're moving rods, we fill the old holes in the same visit.

Common problems

Common curtain rod problems, and how we fix them

  • Brackets pulling out

    Plastic plugs can't handle heavy curtains being pulled daily. We move brackets onto studs where possible, or use toggle or metal hollow-wall anchors rated for the load, and patch the old holes.

  • Concrete and steel lintels

    Above many condo windows there's concrete or steel instead of wood. We use the right bits and concrete or metal fasteners, and vacuum at the drill so dust stays off the sill and floor.

  • Sagging wide rods

    Long rods bow in the middle without support. We add centre brackets at the right spacing, with passing rings or brackets where curtains need to travel the full width.

  • Crooked-looking rods

    Windows, ceilings and floors in older homes are rarely perfectly level. We check them all and level to the line that looks right in the room, talking it through with you first.

  • Old holes after moving rods

    Moving a rod higher and wider makes a window look bigger, but it leaves old holes behind. We fill and touch up small holes in the same visit.

  • Light leaks with blackouts

    Blackout curtains work best on a wrap-around rod or with returns at the sides. We position brackets to minimize gaps and anchor for the extra weight of lined panels.

Our process

How we install curtain rods

  1. Measure & plan

    We confirm rod height, how far it extends past the frame, and where centre supports go, using your curtain length as a guide.

  2. Find solid backing

    Studs, concrete or lintels are located and marked so each bracket goes where it will hold.

  3. Mount & level

    Brackets go up with the right fasteners, the rod is levelled and set, and finials and rings are fitted.

  4. Test & tidy

    We slide the curtains across to check smooth operation, fill any old holes and vacuum the dust.

FAQ

Curtain rod installation questions

Do you supply curtain rods?

We install the rods you provide. If you need a recommendation, we can suggest reliable options for your window size and curtain weight.

Can you drill into concrete or steel lintels?

Yes. We use the correct bits and anchors for concrete, masonry and steel, and clean up after drilling. This is common above condo windows across downtown Toronto.

How high and wide should my curtain rod be?

A good starting point is 10 to 15 cm (4 to 6 inches) above the window frame, or higher to make the window look taller, and 15 to 25 cm (6 to 10 inches) past each side so open panels stack beside the glass instead of covering it. Your curtain length often decides the final height.

Can you hang heavy blackout curtains in a condo?

Yes. We anchor brackets into concrete or framing so they handle the weight, use a centre support on wide windows, and confirm elevator bookings with management when needed.

Can you patch the old holes?

We fill and touch up small holes when relocating rods during the same visit. Larger damage can be repaired as part of our drywall repair service.

What should I have ready before you arrive?

Have the rods, brackets and curtains on hand. With the panels there we can set the height so the hem sits right at the floor. Clear space in front of each window and move anything fragile off the sill.
