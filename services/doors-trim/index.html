Doors that swing right and trim that looks finished

Toronto homes move. Seasonal humidity swings, settling framing and older houses that were never quite square all show up at the doors first: a slab that rubs at the top corner, a latch you have to lean on, a gap opening up under the casing. Trim takes the rest of the abuse, from vacuum-chipped baseboards to casing split by a door handle.

ReadyFix works out why a door is misbehaving before reaching for a plane, because most sticking doors are hinge problems, not wood problems. For trim, we match existing profiles where we can, cut joints tight, and fill, caulk and sand so everything is ready for paint.

Common problems

Common door and trim problems, and how we fix them

  • Sticking or rubbing doors

    A door that rubs at the top corner on the latch side usually has a loose top hinge. We drive longer screws into the framing first. Only if it still rubs do we plane, then seal the bare edge.

  • Latches that won't catch

    When the latch misses the strike, we adjust or reposition the strike plate, deepen the mortise if needed, and fill the old screw holes so the door closes with light pressure.

  • Doors that drift

    A door that swings open or shut on its own is hanging out of plumb. We adjust or shim the hinges to bring it back into line so it stays where you leave it.

  • Closet doors

    Bi-folds that jump the track and sliders that drag get new pivots, guides or rollers where needed, then are adjusted so they run straight and close evenly.

  • Damaged baseboards and casing

    Chipped or water-swollen sections are cut out and replaced with a matching profile, with tight joints, set nails, filled holes and fresh caulk lines so the repair blends in.

  • Drafty exterior doors

    Worn weatherstripping and door sweeps are replaced and adjustable thresholds are tuned, so the door seals evenly and winter drafts stay outside.

Our process

How we repair doors and trim

  1. Diagnose

    We check the hinges, strike and frame first to find out why a door sticks or won't latch, so the fix lasts.

  2. Protect the area

    Drop sheets and floor protection go down, and a HEPA vacuum handles dust from cutting, sanding and planing.

  3. Adjust, repair or replace

    Hinges are tightened or shimmed, strikes aligned, doors planed only where needed, and damaged trim cut out and replaced.

  4. Fill, caulk & test

    Nail holes are filled and joints caulked and sanded paint-ready, and every door is opened and closed before we clean up.

FAQ

Door and trim questions

Can you match my existing trim profile?

We stock common profiles and can source close matches. If an exact match isn't available, we'll talk through options, such as replacing a full wall run or room so the difference isn't noticeable.

Why does my door stick in summer but not in winter?

Wood takes on moisture in humid months and swells slightly, so a door with tight clearances starts to rub. We correct the hinges first, then plane only what's needed and seal the edge so it doesn't keep soaking up moisture.

Do you replace door hardware?

Yes, we install new hinges, handles and locks and align them for smooth latching. For deadbolts, entry handlesets and smart locks, see our lock replacement service.

Can you work in occupied homes and condo suites?

Absolutely. We use drop cloths and HEPA vacuums, confirm elevator bookings with management in condos, and tidy up before we leave.

Do you paint the trim afterwards?

We leave new and repaired trim filled, caulked and sanded so it's ready for paint. Ask about touch-ups when you request a quote; for full rooms we're happy to coordinate with your painter.

What should I send before the visit?

For sticking doors, a short video of the door closing helps us diagnose it. For trim, send a photo of the profile end-on and a rough length of the damaged section. Clearing furniture away from the baseboard runs saves time on the day.
